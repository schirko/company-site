For ranches and anyone renting out grass

Grazing Planner

How much grass will my pasture grow this season, and how many head can it carry?

What It Does

  • Forecasts this season's grass for every Nebraska county from satellite estimates and the weather so far, with an 80% range
  • Head that can graze May to October with enough grass 9 years in 10, and the chance your own herd runs short
  • Green-up date in spring, and a July 1 and August 1 check: keep, move or sell
  • Tested season by season on 2011-2025, and against Grass-Cast, the public forecast from USDA and partners

No sign-in needed to try it. Nebraska counties for now; drawing your own pasture comes later.

A finding from the work behind Grazing Planner, with its source and its limits.

Where the Number Comes From

Each county's grass is the Rangeland Analysis Platform's satellite estimate of forage grown on its grassland (cropland masked out). "Normal" is the county's average over the 10 seasons before. The forecast learns from every earlier season how the weather so far, and the grass already grown, relate to how the season ends; its range comes from how far off it was in those seasons. Head counts use the standard animal-unit-month arithmetic with a 25% harvest on native range.

Source: Rangeland Analysis Platform (USDA Agricultural Research Service), NASA POWER weather. How we test Grazing Planner.
