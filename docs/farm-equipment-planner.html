How Farm Equipment Planner Works

From the data to your decision, in four steps.

  1. Step 1 The Data

    What it reads

    • Your machines, acres and crops
    • Your county's real falls since 2000 (NASA rainfall)
    • ASABE and Iowa State machinery costs

  2. Step 2 The Method

    How it works it out

    • Cost to own and run, by the published method
    • Each fall replayed day by day
    • Thousands of simulated seasons

  3. Step 3 The Answer

    What you get

    Tested: Matches all 64 rows of Iowa State's field capacity table

    • Cost per acre and per bushel
    • Own, lease or hire, side by side
    • The harvest bottleneck

  4. Step 4 Your Decision

    What it helps you choose

    • Buy new, buy used or hire custom
    • The right size of machine
    • Keep or trade

Where the Number Comes From

A day counts as workable when it had under 2.5 mm of rain and under 12.5 mm over the two days before, the same rule the Equipment Planner uses to price harvest delays. We count those days from October 1 to November 30 in every fall since 2000: the typical fall is the median, the wet fall the 10th percentile. It counts rain only, not snow or frozen ground.
