Herd Planner

  • Prices every animal at your own sale barn from live USDA auction reports, with a range tested on sales the model never saw

For corn and soybean growers and advisers

Yield Predictor

  • Predicts county corn and soybean yields from 26 seasons of USDA yields, NASA daily weather, soil ratings and irrigation
  • Tested the hard way: on whole districts and future years it never saw, and on Iowa after learning only from Nebraska
  • Maps where the model is strong and where it misses, so you know when to trust it
  • Shows which weather matters for which crop, and where: August rain decides soybeans, and a long dry spell hurts Nebraska corn but not Iowa's

No account needed. Corn and soybeans, Nebraska and Iowa.

For ranches and anyone renting out grass

Grazing Planner

  • Forecasts this season's grass for every Nebraska county from satellite estimates and the weather so far, with an 80% range
  • Head that can graze May to October with enough grass 9 years in 10, and the chance your own herd runs short
  • Green-up date in spring, and a July 1 and August 1 check: keep, move or sell
  • Tested season by season on 2011-2025, and against Grass-Cast, the public forecast from USDA and partners

Why We Built This

Drought Is Why These Apps Exist

Herd Planner started with the drought. From 2020 to 2023, dry years cut the corn, the hay and the grass across cattle country. With too little to feed, ranchers sold cows, and the country's beef herd got smaller every year. I wanted tools that help a family see a dry year coming and decide early: how many head the grass can carry, how much hay they'll be short, and which cattle to sell first.

Not all of the herd's drop was drought: the cattle cycle and prices played a part too.

  1. Drought Spreads

    By March 2022, 61% of the lower 48 was in drought, covering about 80% of the beef herd.

    U.S. Drought Monitor, via Drovers, Mar 14, 2022

  2. Crops and Hay Come Up Short

    Nebraska, 2022: corn 165 bu/acre, down 29 from 2021. Alfalfa hay down 34%.

    USDA NASS, via UNL CropWatch, Jan 13, 2023

  3. Feed Stays Short

    May 2023: U.S. hay stocks 26% below the 10-year average. Nebraska pasture 68% poor or very poor.

    Derrell Peel, Oklahoma State University Extension, May 16, 2023

  4. Ranchers Sell Cows

    2022: beef cow slaughter up 12.3%. Cows and heifers were over half of all cattle slaughtered, the most since 1986.

    Derrell Peel, Oklahoma State University Extension, Nov 28, 2022

  5. The Herd Shrinks

    31.7 million beef cows in 2019. 27.6 million in 2026.

    USDA NASS Cattle reports, Jan 2020 to Jan 2026
